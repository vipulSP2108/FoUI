Primary Waste Management Operator (P1) is responsible for managing medical waste disposal at the IIT Gandhinagar Medical Centre. The waste generated at the centre is segregated into four categories based on its nature and potential health hazards, with each category assigned a specific colour-coded bin (as documented in the on-site guide):

- Blue Bin – Sharps Medical Waste: This bin is used for disposing of sharp medical instruments such as needles, syringes (without liquid), blades, lancets, and other sharp objects that may cause injuries.
- Black Bin – Chemical Waste: This category includes expired medicines, discarded chemicals, disinfectants, and other pharmaceutical or chemical waste. Empty fluid medicine containers and medicines that can no longer be used are also collected separately, as per the centre's waste management practices.
- Yellow Bin – Human and Anatomical Waste: This bin is designated for waste generated during wound cleaning and other medical procedures involving blood or potentially infectious biological material. This includes soiled dressings, blood-stained materials, body fluids, and other potentially infectious waste.
- Red Bin – Contaminated Plastic Waste: This category includes contaminated plastic medical items such as gloves, IV sets, catheters, tubing, and other disposable plastic materials used during medical procedures.

The recent redesign and renovation of the Medical Centre have significantly improved the way medical waste is managed from an operational logistics standpoint. Previously, the space available for placing waste bins was limited, and bins were not designated for different types of waste. Following the renovation, designated waste bins have been allocated in every patient room.

Outside the Medical Centre, four large bins corresponding to the different waste categories are used for the temporary storage of biomedical waste collected during daily operations. The collected waste is stored in these bins and handed over to an authorised biomedical waste disposal agency every three days for further treatment and disposal.

In addition to these four bins, Primary Waste Operator P1 has arranged a separate large black bin outside the Medical Centre. This bin is specifically used for medicines that have expired or can no longer be used due to reasons such as spillage or other forms of contamination. This ensures that unusable medicines are collected separately rather than being disposed of along with other categories of biomedical waste.

Another significant operational adjustment following the renovation has been the expansion of support staff during peak hours. Previously, Operator P1 managed the entire responsibility alone. However, the facility now has an evening co-worker (Support Staff P2) who works from 5:00 PM to 9:00 PM. The co-worker assists with waste management activities, particularly during the evening shift and in situations requiring additional support or emergency assistance.

Apart from duties at the Medical Centre, Operator P1 is also responsible for managing shredding activities carried out in the adjacent area, as well as overseeing waste management in the consulting rooms located near the Medical Centre.

During the interaction, Operator P1 expressed genuine satisfaction with the work and role. Despite the responsibilities involved in handling and segregating different categories of waste, Operator P1 reported high comfort with the duties. The physical infrastructure improvements and the addition of the evening co-worker have helped make operational work more organised and manageable.
